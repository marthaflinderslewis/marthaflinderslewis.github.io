Hello! I am Assistant Professor of Neurosymbolic AI in the Institute of Logic, Language, and Computation, University of Amsterdam.

Quick links: [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=ZMC6TIAAAAAJ&hl=en); [DBLP](https://dblp.org/pid/133/2000.html)

How are humans able to abstract from their experiences and understand new situations? How do we form novel concepts from what we already know? I use approaches across multiple disciplines to answer questions like these.
I work with a wonderful team of PhD, Masters, and Bachelors students. You can see more about our work at the [CALM lab](https://sites.google.com/view/calm-lab).

## Research Interests
My research is at the intersection of artificial intelligence (AI) and natural language processing (NLP), and is organised across three interrelated strands:

**Compositionality:** Humans are extremely good at combining concepts to create new ones, or to interpret novel situations they have not previously encountered. Think of an orange fluffy bicycle. You can immediately do this, even though you’ve probably never seen one. My work looks at how human compositional abilities can be applied and modelled in AI. Examples are: [integrating grammar with conceptual spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.08314), and a [hierarchical approach to concept composition](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0004370216300492).

**Abstraction:** Part of what makes humans able to apply concepts in new situations is their ability to abstract from specifics. My work in this area looks at the ability of large language models to process abstractions through [metaphor](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.372/), showing that [metaphor interpretation is a challenging task](openreview.net/forum?id=uyTL5Bvosj) for these models, and in building new compositional models for this kind of task (see e.g. [here](aclanthology.org/2020.conll-1.21/) and [here](doi.org/10.4204/EPTCS.406.9)). I also look at the abstract reasoning ability of large language models and find that his breaks down in unfamiliar contexts.

**Reasoning:** Humans are further able to apply abstraction and composition in reasoning tasks. My work in this area uses theoretically motivated and interdisciplinary ideas to build word representations that can be used for reasoning tasks (e.g. [here](collegepublications.co.uk/ifcolog/?00041) and [here](doi.org/10.17791/jcs.2021.22.3.311), and in understanding the capabilities of deep neural models in reasoning tasks such as [analogical reasoning](https://openreview.net/forum?id=t5cy5v9wph) and [visual reasoning](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-eacl.101/).
